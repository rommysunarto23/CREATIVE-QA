# Process & Selection Notes

## Final Selection

### 01 — Hero Product Shot

**Selected:** [`assets/final/01-hero-product-shot.png`](assets/final/01-hero-product-shot.png)

Why it was selected:

- Strongest product presence
- Clear AURELIS branding
- Rich luxury environment without distracting from the bottle
- Warm lighting, stone, navy, and gold effectively establish the campaign palette
- Works best as the visual anchor for the rest of the series

**Alternate:** [`assets/alternates/hero-alternate-minimal.png`](assets/alternates/hero-alternate-minimal.png)

The alternate is cleaner and more minimal, but the product has less visual weight and the large dark-blue background element competes more strongly for attention.

---

### 02 — Lifestyle Editorial Shot

**Selected:** [`assets/final/02-lifestyle-editorial-shot.png`](assets/final/02-lifestyle-editorial-shot.png)

Why it was selected:

- Places the product convincingly within a luxury vanity environment
- Uses props to support the skincare context without overwhelming the bottle
- Maintains the established warm ivory, navy, and gold visual language
- Adds storytelling and aspirational brand context
- Serves a clearly different commercial purpose from the hero image

---

### 03 — Macro Detail Shot

**Selected:** [`assets/final/03-macro-detail-shot.png`](assets/final/03-macro-detail-shot.png)

Why it was selected:

- Uses true close-cropped editorial framing
- Emphasizes the frosted glass texture, cap finish, and gold neck ring
- Introduces visual energy through a diagonal composition
- Feels tactile, refined, and premium
- Completes the campaign with a focus on materials and craftsmanship

**Rejected first attempt:** [`assets/alternates/detail-alternate-too-hero-like.png`](assets/alternates/detail-alternate-too-hero-like.png)

The first close-up was visually polished but still read as a full-product hero shot with a tighter crop. It did not create enough visual distinction from the main campaign image. The prompt was therefore revised to explicitly request a true macro composition with material-focused framing.

---

## Final Campaign Logic

```text
Hero
  ↓
Establish product + visual identity

Lifestyle
  ↓
Place the product within an aspirational brand context

Macro Detail
  ↓
Highlight craftsmanship, materials, and premium finish
```

The final set intentionally varies in framing and commercial purpose while maintaining a recognizable visual and brand language.

## What the Iteration Demonstrated

The rejected close-up was useful because it exposed a distinction between **a visually attractive output** and **an output that actually fulfills the requested deliverable**. The solution was not to generate more of the same image, but to change the camera-language instruction from “close-up” to a material-focused macro composition.

## Notes for Future Projects

- Establish one visual anchor before generating campaign variations.
- Define which product characteristics are immutable and which can vary.
- Give each image a distinct commercial purpose rather than simply changing the crop.
- Reject visually attractive generations when they do not satisfy the brief.
- Minimize unnecessary model-generated label copy when exact packaging fidelity matters.
- Distinguish recognizable visual consistency from pixel-perfect package reproduction.
