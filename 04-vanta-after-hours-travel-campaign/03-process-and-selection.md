# Process & Selection Notes — VANTA / AFTER HOURS

## Goal

Create a three-image campaign that feels like one continuous late-night journey rather than three independent luggage advertisements.

The final sequence needed to preserve recognizable traveler, product, wardrobe, and visual-world continuity while deliberately changing camera language as the narrative progressed.

The selection process prioritized:

1. Narrative progression
2. Product visibility
3. Recognizable traveler continuity
4. Recognizable luggage continuity
5. Camera-language variation
6. Human-product interaction
7. Cinematic atmosphere
8. Commercial usefulness
9. Brief adherence

## Stage 1 — Departure / Hero Anchor

**Selected:** `assets/final/01-departure-hero-anchor.png`

### Why It Was Selected

- Strong introduction of the traveler and product
- Clear premium-travel context
- Believable luggage proportions
- Natural hand / telescopic-handle interaction
- Distinct deep-blue and amber nighttime palette
- Strong use of reflective surfaces and environmental depth
- Product remains commercially visible without becoming a static catalog packshot

### Campaign Role

The Departure Hero establishes the traveler, **VANTA Carry-On 01**, nocturnal environment, and emotional tone of the campaign.

Its relatively frontal composition works as an opening frame because the audience first needs to understand who is traveling, what product is central to the story, and what visual world the campaign occupies.

This image became the primary traveler, luggage, wardrobe, and visual-world reference for subsequent generations.

## Stage 2 — In Transit / Rejected First Attempt

**Rejected:** `assets/alternates/rejected-in-transit-too-similar.png`

### Why It Was Rejected

The first In Transit attempt was visually strong, but its camera angle, walking direction, suitcase position, body orientation, and framing remained too similar to the Departure Hero.

The traveler was again presented from a largely frontal perspective, with the suitcase occupying a similar relationship to the body. As a result, the frame read more like another hero image in a slightly different terminal setting than a true second chapter in the journey.

The issue was not general visual quality. The issue was narrative function.

The frame did not create enough change in camera geometry, movement direction, or composition to communicate that the journey had progressed.

**Lesson:** a visually attractive image can still fail the brief if it does not advance the narrative.

## Stage 3 — In Transit / Camera-Language Correction

The In Transit prompt was rewritten to make the compositional change explicit.

The revision introduced:

- Side-profile or three-quarter side view
- Left-to-right movement
- A camera position perpendicular to the traveler's direction
- A wider environmental frame
- More visible negative space
- A suitcase trailing behind rather than sitting directly beside the traveler
- Tracking-shot language
- Background and reflection cues that reinforce directional movement

This changed the campaign from repeating the hero composition to deliberately controlling the visual grammar of the second scene.

## Stage 4 — In Transit / Final

**Selected:** `assets/final/02-in-transit-motion.png`

### Why It Was Selected

- Strong lateral movement
- Clear left-to-right travel direction
- Suitcase trails behind the traveler instead of repeating the hero placement
- Environmental depth supports the sense of progression
- Reflective flooring reinforces the established visual world
- Traveler and luggage remain clearly readable
- Camera language is meaningfully different from the hero

### Campaign Role

The In Transit image shifts the campaign from introduction to active progression.

Where the hero establishes the traveler, product, and world, this frame introduces direction, stride, and forward movement.

The resulting camera change is central to the campaign structure:

**Frontal hero → lateral motion**

## Stage 5 — Arrival / Detail Narrative

**Selected:** `assets/final/03-arrival-detail-narrative.png`

### Why It Was Selected

- Moves away from full-body framing
- Focuses on the hand, handle, suitcase, shoes, and material detail
- Preserves the established nighttime visual world
- Arrival-zone / curbside cues create narrative closure
- A more intimate crop gives the campaign a quieter final rhythm
- Product remains visually important
- Warmer practical lighting supports the feeling of an endpoint without leaving the established navy / charcoal / amber palette

### Campaign Role

The Arrival image resolves the story through a closer, more controlled product-and-human interaction.

Instead of introducing another walking frame, the composition slows the narrative down and narrows the visual focus. The traveler's grip, luggage surface, wheels, footwear, reflections, and nearby vehicle cues create a final moment that feels more intimate than the first two assets.

This completes the camera-language progression:

**Frontal hero → lateral motion → intimate arrival detail**

## Final Campaign Logic

```text
Departure
  ↓
Introduce traveler + product + world

In Transit
  ↓
Show movement + journey progression

Arrival
  ↓
Resolve through intimate product interaction
```

The final sequence is designed to read as:

**one traveler + one carry-on + one nocturnal journey**

## Visual QA Framework

The final images were evaluated against:

1. **Narrative role** — Does the frame advance or resolve the story?
2. **Product recognition** — Does the luggage remain clearly identifiable?
3. **Traveler continuity** — Does the traveler remain recognizably connected across the sequence?
4. **Human realism** — Do anatomy, grip, posture, and body-product interaction remain believable?
5. **Product geometry** — Are shell proportions, handle, wheels, and scale credible?
6. **Camera differentiation** — Does each image use a meaningfully different visual language?
7. **World continuity** — Does the scene remain within the nocturnal navy / charcoal / amber campaign universe?
8. **Commercial role** — Does the image provide a distinct function within the campaign rather than merely repeating an existing frame?

## Final Campaign Structure

| Frame | Camera Language | Narrative Function |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Departure / Hero | Frontal or near-frontal campaign framing | Introduce traveler, product, and world |
| In Transit / Motion | Lateral tracking-style movement | Advance the journey |
| Arrival / Detail Narrative | Close, intimate detail framing | Resolve the journey |

## Lessons Carried Forward

- Lock recognizable identity and product design, but deliberately change camera geometry between scenes.
- Do not rely on repeated generations of the same prompt for meaningful compositional variety.
- Use rejected outputs to document why a visually attractive frame can still fail the campaign brief.
- Give every image a distinct narrative and commercial function.
- Treat camera position, movement direction, crop, and product placement as part of the storytelling system.
- Separate generation constraints from portfolio claims about final-output continuity.
