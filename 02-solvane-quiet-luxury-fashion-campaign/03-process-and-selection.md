# Process & Selection Notes

## Goal

The campaign needed to show that recognizable visual continuity could coexist with distinct editorial compositions. Outputs were selected not only for visual appeal, but also for how well they followed the brief.

## Stage 1 — Rejected Warm Direction

The first hero generation produced a strong fashion image but used warm limestone, beige architecture, and golden sunlight.

### Why It Was Rejected

The image was attractive in isolation, but it overlapped too strongly with Project #1 (AURELIS), which already used warm ivory, stone, and golden beauty lighting.

**Lesson:** A visually strong image can still fail the art direction.

Stored as:  
[`assets/alternates/rejected-warm-direction.png`](assets/alternates/rejected-warm-direction.png)

## Stage 2 — Editorial Hero Anchor

The revised hero moved the campaign into a cooler architectural world using grey concrete, smoked glass, dark metal, and neutral daylight.

### Why It Was Selected

- Clear separation from AURELIS
- Strong, recognizable model identity
- Believable tailoring
- Natural handbag proportions
- Cool urban editorial tone
- Strong full-body campaign composition

Stored as:  
[`assets/final/01-editorial-hero-anchor.png`](assets/final/01-editorial-hero-anchor.png)

This image became the **character + wardrobe anchor** for subsequent generations.

## Stage 3 — Urban Movement

Two generations from the same prompt were nearly identical. This showed strong visual continuity with the reference but limited compositional variation.

### Selected Version

The first candidate was selected because the blazer opening, stride, and reflective floor created slightly stronger movement and editorial energy.

Stored as:  
[`assets/final/02-urban-movement.png`](assets/final/02-urban-movement.png)

### Alternate

The second version was kept as an alternate because it remained valid but was visually redundant.

Stored as:  
[`assets/alternates/urban-movement-alternate.png`](assets/alternates/urban-movement-alternate.png)

**Lesson:** When creative alternatives are required, change the camera language or movement direction intentionally rather than relying on repeated generations of the exact same prompt.

## Stage 4 — Tailoring Detail

The final image intentionally changed the camera language from full-body fashion portraiture to a close editorial material study.

### Why It Was Selected

- Strong crop separation from the first two images
- Believable lapel and trouser construction
- Clear fabric and silk texture
- Natural hand interaction
- Recognizable handbag and jewelry continuity
- Maintained the cool SOLVANE environment

Stored as:  
[`assets/final/03-tailoring-detail.png`](assets/final/03-tailoring-detail.png)

## Final Campaign Structure

1. **Editorial Hero** — establishes identity and visual world
2. **Urban Movement** — adds motion and metropolitan storytelling
3. **Tailoring Detail** — shifts attention to material quality and craftsmanship

## Final QA Result

The final three-image set reads as one coordinated campaign while providing enough variation in composition and camera language to avoid feeling repetitive.
